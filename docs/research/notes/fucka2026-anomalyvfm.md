# Setting

Zero-shot anomaly detection and localisation: detect and localise abnormal regions in images of object classes never seen in training, "without access to any in-domain training images" (Abstract). Training uses only an auxiliary dataset; here that dataset is entirely synthetic and generated by the paper (§3, §5.1). Inputs at test time: a single RGB image (resized to 768x768 for the RADIO backbone, §5.2). Outputs: a pixel-wise anomaly map $M_o$ and an image-level anomaly score $A_o$ (§4). Evaluation: nine industrial datasets (MVTec AD, VisA, BTAD, MPDD, Real-IAD, KSDD, KSDD2, DAGM, DTD-Synthetic) and nine medical datasets (HeadCT, BrainMRI, BR35H, ISIC, ClinicDB, ColonDB, Kvasir, Endo, TN3K); metrics follow AdaCLIP: image-level AUROC and F1-max, pixel-level AUROC and F1-max (§5.1). Competing methods are trained on a real auxiliary set (the VisA test set when evaluated on MVTec AD, otherwise the MVTec AD test set), whereas AnomalyVFM "is trained solely on automatically generated data" (§5.1).

# Core idea

Two ingredients that together make a purely visual foundation model (VFM: DINOv2, DINOv3, RADIO) a competitive zero-shot detector, after the authors argue VFM-based zero-shot AD lagged VLM-based AD because of (i) low diversity of existing auxiliary AD datasets and (ii) shallow adaptation that only trains a small head (Abstract; §1).

(1) Three-stage synthetic dataset generation (§3). Stage 1: an anomaly-free object image $I=G(p)$ from a prompt template ("A close-up photo of [Object] for industrial visual inspection. Top-down view. Centered. [Texture] background."), with $G$ the flow-matching model FLUX, and [Object]/[Texture] drawn from LLM-generated lists of 100 objects and 50 backgrounds (Eq. 1). Stage 2: sample an anomaly location on the IS-Net foreground mask, form a rectangle $R$ with $w\sim U(w_{min},w_{max})$, $h\sim U(h_{min},h_{max})$ (Eq. 2), and inpaint an LLM-listed [Anomaly] description inside $R$ with RePaint-style iterative generation (no inpainting-specific model). Stage 3: DINOv2 features of $I$ and $I_a$ are compared by cosine distance to obtain a distance map $M_d$; $D=\max M_d$; the sample is kept only if $D>T$, and thresholding $M_d$ gives the pseudo-mask $M$ (§3).

(2) Parameter-efficient adaptation (§4). A LoRA block is inserted in the query, value and output projection of the attention in every transformer block (rank 64); the final-block features are reshaped into a map and passed through a small convolutional decoder (two up-sampling blocks of conv + GroupNorm + ReLU + bilinear upsampling, then a final conv that outputs both the segmentation map $M_o$ and a confidence map $c$); the [CLS] token goes through a linear layer to give the image score $A_o$. Losses: image-level Focal; segmentation base loss $L_{base}=L_1(M_o,M_{GT})+\beta L_{focal}(M_o,M_{GT})$ with $\beta=5$ (Eq. 3); confidence weighting $L_{seg}=L_{base}\cdot C-\alpha\log C$ with $C=1+\exp(c)$, $\alpha=0.1$ (Eq. 4); total $L=L_{seg}+L_{img}$ (§4). Training: 500 iterations, batch 32, AdamW, lr $10^{-4}$, RADIOv2.5 ViT-L/16 backbone for most experiments (§5.2). A few-shot variant simply fine-tunes the zero-shot model for 50 more iterations on the shots (§5.5).

# Claimed contributions

- C1: "As our main contribution, we introduce AnomalyVFM, an effective framework that transforms any pretrained VFM into a competitive zero-shot anomaly detector using a parameter-efficient adaptation scheme and a synthetically generated dataset containing diverse data." (§1, first bullet)
- C2: "As our secondary contribution, we propose a scalable scheme for generating synthetic anomaly detection datasets ... three-stage synthesis process that leverages modern generative models to produce diverse object instances, realistic local defects, and automatic feature-based verification to ensure data quality." (§1, second bullet)
- C3: Zero-shot accuracy: "surpassing the previous best zero-shot anomaly detection methods by a significant 3.3 percentage points (p. p.) in image-level AUROC and 0.9 p. p. in pixel-level AUROC" on nine industrial benchmarks; average image-level AUROC 94.1% with RADIO (Abstract; §1; Table 2).
- C4: Medical generalisation without medical fine-tuning (§1; Table 3), and few-shot competitiveness after 50 fine-tuning iterations: matches or surpasses INP-Former (§1; §5.5; Table 4).
- C5: Model-agnostic: works with DINOv2, DINOv3 and RADIO; on average +6.1 p.p. image-level and +10.7 p.p. pixel-level AUROC over a head-only baseline (§5.3, Table 1).

Calibration notes: the "model-agnostic" claim is tested on three ViT backbones only ("any pretrained VFM with a transformer backbone", §1); the few-shot claim is not uniform (INP-Former is better than AnomalyVFM at 2- and 4-shot VisA: 94.6 / 96.4 vs 94.2 / 94.5, Table 4).

# Assumptions

1. **(Hard)** The generative model produces plausible, mostly local defects on isolated centred objects ("top-down view, centered") with a salient foreground; the pipeline relies on a salient-object network (IS-Net) to choose the defect location (§3). Removing foreground selection costs 1.4 p.p. image-level and 5.8 p.p. pixel-level AUROC (§6).
2. **(Hard)** Generated-image anomaly presence is verified only through DINOv2 feature distance between $I$ and $I_a$; about 30% of samples are rejected at $T=0.3$ (Supplementary §E). Without filtering, pixel-level AUROC drops 14.6 p.p. (§6).
3. **(Hard)** Medical data cannot be synthesised by the off-the-shelf generators ("failed to output realistic medical images", Supplementary §A), so medical performance is cross-domain transfer from the industrial synthetic set.
4. **(Soft)** Anomaly rectangles of 50-350 px on 1024x1024 images (average anomalous area 2.52% vs 4.39% in MVTec AD, Supplementary §D) — defect scale prior is baked into the training set.
5. **(Soft)** [Object] tags were manually screened to exclude tags present in evaluation test sets (Supplementary §B); [Anomaly] and [Texture] tags were left as generated.

# Failure regime

Authors' stated limitation: dataset generation takes about one day on an A100, versus about two hours of training (Supplementary §A, §6). Medical image-level classification is "competitive" rather than leading (Table 3: average image-level AUROC 94.0 vs Bayes-PFL 94.9 and AdaCLIP† 94.6, the latter trained with auxiliary medical data). Pixel-level F1-max trails AdaCLIP (44.3 vs 51.0, Table 2). Dataset generation failure cases without anomalies still pass the filter (Supplementary Fig. 1). The paper reports training competing VLM methods on the synthetic set gives no consistent gain (e.g. AdaCLIP drops 2.5 p.p. image-level AUROC, Supplementary Table 1), which they read as VLM methods not being data-diversity limited. Logical/count anomalies are not evaluated; the only mention of "logical" is in the reference list.

# Numerical sensitivity

Hyperparameters: LoRA rank 64 (rank 32 loses 0.3 / 0.3 p.p., rank 128 changes -0.0 / -0.3, Supp. §E), $\beta=5$, $\alpha=0.1$, filtering threshold $T=0.3$ (image-level AUROC "quite robust", pixel-level "more reliant on a correct choice of a threshold", Supp. §E; Fig. 3 axis spans 0.15-0.40), 10,000 images (performance rises steadily from 100 to 10,000), 100 [Object] tags (20 tags ~ trained-on-MVTec performance), 768x768 input for RADIO (§5.2). Model size: ViT-L best (ViT-B -1.8 / -1.2 image/pixel AUROC; ViT-H -0.6 image-level, and pixel-level -1.2 in the Supp. §E text but -0.8 in Supp. Table 3 — internal inconsistency `?`). Footprint: 345.8 M parameters, of which 35.4 M are trainable; ~2 h on one A100 (§6). Inference 20.5 ms/sample on an A100 vs 82.4 ms (AdaCLIP) and 208.5 ms (Bayes-PFL), measured with EfficientAD's protocol (Table 6).

# Applicability

- Use when: zero-shot detection on arbitrary industrial object classes (no normal images) with a strong VFM backbone; a GPU for roughly one day of synthetic-data generation is acceptable; masks are needed (pixel maps come out of the same model).
- Don't use when: medical imaging is the main domain and extra performance is needed (generation fails there); anomalies are large-area, global or logical rather than small local defects; non-transformer backbones are required.
- Compared against (Tables 2-4): SAA, WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP, AA-CLIP, Bayes-PFL, FAPrompt (zero-shot); PatchCore, WinCLIP+, PromptAD, INP-Former (few-shot).
