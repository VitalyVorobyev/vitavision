# Setting

Self-supervised pretraining of an image representation from unlabeled RGB images, evaluated primarily by a linear classifier trained on the frozen representation ("linear evaluation protocol"). Input: unlabeled images, principally ImageNet ILSVRC-2012 train split (no labels used during pretraining; Places365-Standard is used in Appendix E as a secondary confirmation dataset). Output: an encoder $f_\theta$ producing a representation $y_\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{2048}$ (for a ResNet-50 with width multiplier $1\times$, taken after the final average-pooling layer) for each image. At the end of training everything except $f_\theta$ is discarded; $y_\theta = f_\theta(v)$ is the representation used for all downstream evaluation (§3.1, Fig. 2 caption). No architectural constraint on $f_\theta$ beyond the ResNet family tested; no memory bank, no queue, and — the paper's central claim — no negative pairs at all (Introduction).

# Core idea

BYOL trains two networks with identical architecture but separate weights that "interact and learn from each other" (Introduction): an **online network** (weights $\theta$) and a **target network** (weights $\xi$). Each is composed of three stages — an encoder $f$, a projector $g$, and (online only) a predictor $q$ — except the target network has no predictor, making the architecture asymmetric between the two branches (§3.1). Given an image $x \sim \mathcal{D}$ and two augmentation distributions $\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{T}'$, two augmented views $v = t(x)$, $v' = t'(x)$ are drawn ($t \sim \mathcal{T}$, $t' \sim \mathcal{T}'$). The online network computes $y_\theta = f_\theta(v)$, $z_\theta = g_\theta(y_\theta)$; the target network computes $y'_\xi = f_\xi(v')$, $z'_\xi = g_\xi(y'_\xi)$. The online network then outputs a prediction $q_\theta(z_\theta)$ of $z'_\xi$; both are $\ell_2$-normalized, and BYOL minimizes the normalized mean-squared-error (§3.1, Eq. 2):

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\xi} \triangleq \left\| \overline{q_\theta}(z_\theta) - \bar{z}'_\xi \right\|_2^2 = 2 - 2 \cdot \frac{\langle q_\theta(z_\theta), z'_\xi \rangle}{\lVert q_\theta(z_\theta)\rVert_2 \cdot \lVert z'_\xi \rVert_2}
$$

The loss is **symmetrized**: $v'$ is separately fed to the online network and $v$ to the target network to compute $\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta,\xi}$, and the total objective is $\mathcal{L}^{\text{BYOL}}_{\theta,\xi} = \mathcal{L}_{\theta,\xi} + \widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta,\xi}$ (§3.1). At each step, only $\theta$ is updated by gradient descent on $\mathcal{L}^{\text{BYOL}}_{\theta,\xi}$; $\xi$ receives **no gradient** — a stop-gradient is applied on the entire target branch (Fig. 2 caption; "with respect to $\theta$ only, but not $\xi$"). Instead $\xi$ is updated as an exponential moving average (EMA) of $\theta$ (§3.1, Eq. 1):

$$
\xi \leftarrow \tau \xi + (1-\tau)\theta
$$

BYOL's full update dynamics are (§3.1, Eqs. 3 & 1): $\theta \leftarrow \text{optimizer}(\theta, \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}^{\text{BYOL}}_{\theta,\xi}, \eta)$; $\xi \leftarrow \tau\xi + (1-\tau)\theta$. In the main experiments $\tau$ starts at $\tau_{\text{base}} = 0.996$ and is annealed toward 1 over training via a cosine schedule (§3.3): $\tau \triangleq 1 - (1-\tau_{\text{base}}) \cdot (\cos(\pi k/K) + 1)/2$, with $k$ the current step and $K$ the total number of steps.

**Why BYOL claims not to collapse.** The paper is explicit that this is an empirical, not a proven, claim: "we hypothesize that there is no loss $L_{\theta,\xi}$ such that BYOL's dynamics is a gradient descent on $L$ jointly over $\theta,\xi$" — drawing an analogy to GANs, where no loss is jointly minimized w.r.t. both players (§3.2). "While BYOL's dynamics still admit undesirable equilibria, we did not observe convergence to such equilibria in our experiments" (§3.2) — this is stated as an empirical observation, not a guarantee. Under the additional assumption of a near-optimal predictor $q^\star(z_\theta) = \mathbb{E}[z'_\xi \mid z_\theta]$ (Eq. 4), the paper shows BYOL's online updates follow, in expectation, the gradient of the conditional variance $\sum_i \text{Var}(z'_{\xi,i} \mid z_\theta)$ (Eq. 5, derivation in Appendix H); since discarding information from the online projection cannot decrease this conditional variance, and a constant $z_\theta$ maximizes it, the argument is that collapsed constant equilibria are unstable under this dynamic (§3.2). The paper's own ablations (Table 5) show empirically that removing either the predictor or the target network causes collapse — "the additional predictor is critical to prevent collapse" (§5, "Relationship with Mean Teacher") — and that near-optimality of the predictor, not the target network per se, appears to be the load-bearing property: replacing the moving-average target with an *optimal linear predictor* (computed by per-batch linear regression) avoids collapse without any target network at all, reaching 52.5% top-1, and increasing just the predictor's learning rate reaches 66.5% top-1 without a target network (§5 "Importance of a near-optimal predictor", Appendix I). "This seems to indicate that keeping the predictor near-optimal at all times is important to preventing collapse, which may be one of the roles of BYOL's target network" (§5) — the paper itself flags this as a hypothesis, not a proof.

# Claimed contributions

The Introduction lists three numbered contributions (§1):

- C1: "We introduce BYOL, a self-supervised representation learning method (Section 3) which achieves state-of-the-art results under the linear evaluation protocol on ImageNet without using negative pairs."
- C2: "We show that our learned representation outperforms the state of the art on semi-supervised and transfer benchmarks (Section 4)."
- C3: "We show that BYOL is more resilient to changes in the batch size and in the set of image augmentations compared to its contrastive counterparts (Section 5). In particular, BYOL suffers a much smaller performance drop than SimCLR, a strong contrastive baseline, when only using random crops as image augmentations."

The abstract/Introduction headline numbers backing C1: "BYOL reaches $74.3\%$ top-1 accuracy with a standard ResNet-50 and $79.6\%$ top-1 accuracy with a larger ResNet" (§1).

# Assumptions

1. (Hard) The target network's parameters $\xi$ are never updated by back-propagation — only by the EMA rule (Eq. 1). At $\tau=0$ (instantaneous copy, i.e. "stop gradient of online"), training collapses to 0.3% top-1 (Table 5a).
2. (Hard) A predictor $q_\theta$ must exist on the online branch only — removing the predictor while keeping a target network reproduces an unsupervised Mean-Teacher-style consistency loss, which the paper reports collapses (§5, "Relationship with Mean Teacher"; Table 5b row 7, $\beta=0$, no predictor → collapse).
3. (Soft) $\tau_{\text{base}}$ must sit in a moderate range; $\tau_{\text{base}}=1$ (frozen randomly-initialized target) yields only 18.8% top-1 — much better than the random network alone (1.4%) but far below a moving target — while $\tau_{\text{base}} \in \{0.9, 0.99, 0.999\}$ all exceed 68.4% at 300 epochs, with 0.99 best at that shortened schedule (Table 5a).
4. (Soft) Weight decay is required: "removing the weight decay in either BYOL or SimCLR leads to network divergence" (§5, "Network hyperparameters").
5. (Soft) The image augmentation set follows SimCLR's, augmented with asymmetric Gaussian blur and solarization applied to the two views with different probabilities (Appendix B) — BYOL is reported to be more robust than SimCLR to weakening this set, but robustness is not claimed to be total (C3; §5 "Image augmentations").

# Failure regime

- **No target network, no predictor (SimCLR-equivalent limit).** Setting $\beta=1$ and removing both the predictor and target network recovers a form of the SimCLR objective (§5, "Ablation to contrastive methods"; Table 5b), which does not collapse but tops out at 69.4% top-1 at 300 epochs vs. BYOL's 72.5% under the same 300-epoch ablation regime.
- **Predictor removed, target network kept ($\beta=0$).** Collapses (Table 5b, row 7) — described as "an unsupervised version of MT [Mean Teacher] with no classification loss" (§5).
- **Target network instantaneously copied ($\tau=0$).** "Instantaneously updating the target network ($\tau=0$) destabilizes training, yielding very poor performance" — 0.3% top-1 (§5 "Bootstrapping", Table 5a).
- **Target network frozen ($\tau=1$, random init).** Training is stable but there is no iterative improvement of the target; final representation reaches only 18.8% top-1, though this is still far above the raw random network's own 1.4% (§3, motivating discussion; Table 5a).
- **Small batch size, batch-normalization dependence.** BYOL's performance "remains stable over a wide range of batch sizes from 256 to 4096, and only drops for smaller values due to batch normalization layers in the encoder" (§5 "Batch size") — the residual batch-size sensitivity below 256 is attributed entirely to BatchNorm statistics, not to the loss formulation itself: "the only dependency on batch size in our training pipeline sits within the batch normalization layers" (§5, footnote 7).
- **Augmentation reduced to crops only.** BYOL degrades from 72.5% to 59.4% top-1 (−13.1 points) when image augmentations are reduced to random crops only, vs. SimCLR degrading from 67.9% to 40.3% (−27.6 points) under the identical ablation (§5 "Image augmentations") — BYOL degrades far less, but does still degrade substantially.
- **Color distortion removed specifically.** BYOL loses 9.1 points; SimCLR loses 22.2 points (§5 "Image augmentations", Fig. 3b).
- **No weight decay.** Both BYOL and SimCLR diverge without it (§5 "Network hyperparameters").

# Numerical sensitivity

**Architecture (§3.3):** base encoder ResNet-50 ($1\times$) v1 (post-activation); deeper (50/101/152/200-layer) and wider ($1\times$–$4\times$) ResNets also evaluated. Representation $y$ = output of final average pool, 2048-d at width $1\times$. Projector $g_\theta$ (and identically-shaped target projector $g_\xi$): linear layer → 4096-d hidden, BatchNorm, ReLU, final linear layer → 256-d output. Unlike SimCLR, the MLP's output is **not** batch-normalized. The predictor $q_\theta$ uses the same architecture as $g_\theta$ (linear-4096-BN-ReLU-linear-256), applied only on the online branch.

**Optimization (§3.3):** LARS optimizer with cosine-decay learning-rate schedule (no restarts), 1000 epochs, 10-epoch warm-up. Base learning rate $0.2$, scaled linearly with batch size: $\text{LearningRate} = 0.2 \times \text{BatchSize}/256$. Global weight decay $1.5 \times 10^{-6}$, excluding biases and BatchNorm parameters from both LARS adaptation and weight decay. Target decay rate $\tau_{\text{base}} = 0.996$, annealed toward 1 via the cosine schedule above. Batch size 4096, split over 512 Cloud TPU v3 cores; ResNet-50 ($\times 1$) training takes ≈8 hours at this scale.

**Ablation-regime hyperparameters differ from the main setup (§5):** ablations in §5 use 300 epochs on 64 TPU v3 cores (not 1000 epochs / 512 cores), initial LR 0.3 (not 0.2), batch size 4096, weight decay $10^{-6}$ (not $1.5\times10^{-6}$), and $\tau_{\text{base}}=0.99$ (not 0.996) — chosen because "relative improvements at 100 epochs do not always hold over longer training," so ablations use 300 epochs as a faster-but-representative proxy for the full 1000-epoch regime. **Ablation numbers in this note (Tables 5a/5b, Fig. 3) are under this 300-epoch/$\tau_{\text{base}}=0.99$ regime, not the headline 1000-epoch/$\tau_{\text{base}}=0.996$ setup** — do not conflate the two when citing ablation percentages against Table 1/2/3 headline numbers.

**Image augmentations (Appendix B, Table 6):** random crop (area $\in [8\%, 100\%]$, aspect ratio log-uniform in $[3/4, 4/3]$, resized to $224\times224$ via bicubic interpolation, probability 1.0 for both $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{T}'$); horizontal flip ($p=0.5$, both); color jitter (brightness/contrast max intensity 0.4, saturation max intensity 0.2, hue max intensity 0.1, applied with $p=0.8$, both); color dropping / grayscale ($p=0.2$, both; luma computed as $0.2989r + 0.5870g + 0.1140b$); Gaussian blur (square $23\times23$ kernel at $224\times224$, $\sigma \sim \text{Uniform}[0.1, 2.0]$) — **asymmetric**: $p=1.0$ for view $\mathcal{T}$, $p=0.1$ for view $\mathcal{T}'$; solarization ($x \mapsto x\cdot\mathbb{1}_{\{x<0.5\}} + (1-x)\cdot\mathbb{1}_{\{x\geq0.5\}}$) — **asymmetric**: $p=0.0$ for $\mathcal{T}$, $p=0.2$ for $\mathcal{T}'$.

**Headline linear-evaluation results, ImageNet (Table 1, §4):** BYOL ResNet-50 ($1\times$): **74.3%** top-1 / 91.6% top-5 — "a 1.3% (resp. 0.5%) improvement over the previous self-supervised state of the art [InfoMin Aug., 73.0%/91.1%]"; also compared against SimCLR 69.3%/89.0%, MoCo v2 71.1%, CPC v2 63.8%/85.3%, CMC 66.2%/87.0%, PIRL 63.6%, Local Agg. 60.2%. Supervised baselines cited for context: 76.5% (SimCLR paper's supervised ResNet-50) and 78.9% (a stronger supervised baseline, [75]). Wider/deeper variants: BYOL ResNet-50 ($2\times$, 94M params) 77.4%/93.6% vs. SimCLR ResNet-50 ($2\times$) 74.2%/92.0% and CMC ResNet-50 ($2\times$) 70.6%/89.7%; BYOL ResNet-50 ($4\times$, 375M) 78.6%/94.2%, "similar to the 78.9% of the best supervised baseline... for the same architecture"; BYOL ResNet-200 ($2\times$, 250M) **79.6%/94.8%** — the paper's best result, "which improves over the previous state of the art (76.8%) while using 30% fewer parameters" (§6 Conclusion).

**Semi-supervised fine-tuning, ImageNet (Table 2, §4):** with 1%/10% labeled data — supervised baseline 25.4/56.4 top-1, 48.4/80.4 top-5; SimCLR 48.3/65.6 top-1, 75.5/87.8 top-5; BYOL ResNet-50 **53.2/68.8** top-1, **78.4/89.0** top-5. BYOL ResNet-200 ($2\times$): 71.2/77.7 top-1, 89.5/93.7 top-5. BYOL fine-tuned on 100% of ImageNet labels (ResNet-50) reaches 77.7% top-1 (§4, "Semi-supervised training on ImageNet"; note this is a different number from the ResNet-200 1%/10%-label top-5 figure that shares the digits "77.7" — do not conflate).

**Transfer to 12 classification datasets, linear eval + fine-tuned, ResNet-50 (Table 3, §4):** "BYOL outperforms SimCLR on all benchmarks and the Supervised-IN baseline on 7 of the 12 benchmarks." E.g. linear eval: Food101 75.3 (BYOL) vs. 72.8 (SimCLR repro) vs. 72.3 (Supervised-IN); CIFAR100 78.4 vs. 74.4 vs. 78.3; Birdsnap 57.2 vs. 42.4 vs. 53.7.

**Transfer to other vision tasks (Table 4, §4):** VOC2012 semantic segmentation — BYOL beats Supervised-IN by +1.9 mIoU and SimCLR by +1.1 mIoU. Object detection (Faster R-CNN, VOC trainval2007→test2007, AP50) — BYOL beats Supervised-IN by +3.1 AP50 and SimCLR by +2.3 AP50. NYU v2 depth estimation — BYOL improves the pct.<1.25 metric by +3.5 points over supervised and +1.3 points over SimCLR.

**Batch-size robustness (Fig. 3a, §5):** trained from batch 128 to 4096 with gradient accumulation for smaller batches; "the performance of SimCLR rapidly deteriorates with batch size... In contrast, the performance of BYOL remains stable over a wide range of batch sizes from 256 to 4096, and only drops for smaller values due to batch normalization layers in the encoder."

**Augmentation robustness (Fig. 3b, §5):** removing color distortion: BYOL −9.1 points vs. SimCLR −22.2 points. Crops-only: BYOL 72.5%→59.4% (−13.1) vs. SimCLR 67.9%→40.3% (−27.6).

**Bootstrapping / target-decay ablation (Table 5a, §5, 300-epoch regime):** constant random target ($\tau_{\text{base}}=1$) → 18.8% ± 0.7; moving average $\tau_{\text{base}}=0.999$ → 69.8%; $\tau_{\text{base}}=0.99$ → **72.5%** (best); $\tau_{\text{base}}=0.9$ → 68.4%; stop-gradient-of-online ($\tau=\tau_{\text{base}}=0$, held constant) → 0.3%.

**BYOL/SimCLR unification ablation (Table 5b, §5, 300-epoch regime), via the generalized objective $\text{InfoNCE}^{\alpha,\beta}_\theta$ (Eq. 6–7):** BYOL (predictor ✓, target network ✓, $\beta=0$) → **72.5%** (best); predictor ✓, target ✓, $\beta=1$ (negatives added back without re-tuning temperature) → 70.9%; predictor ✓, no target, $\beta=1$ → 70.7%; SimCLR (no predictor, no target, $\beta=1$) → 69.4%; no predictor, target ✓, $\beta=1$ → 69.1%; three further rows at no predictor / no target with $\beta \in \{0.3, 0.2, 0.1\}$ are listed in the table but their exact top-1 values could not be reliably parsed out of the ar5iv HTML table markup for this note `?` — the qualitative point is nonetheless explicit in prose: "The only variant that performs well without negative examples (i.e., with $\beta=0$) is BYOL." Separately, "Simply adding a target network to SimCLR already improves performance (+1.6 points)" — read against the table this is the 69.4%→70.9%(ish) comparison (predictor-less, target-added row vs. the SimCLR row); the paper uses this to argue the target network has stabilizing value independent of negative-pair mining, and explicitly connects this to MoCo's own use of a momentum encoder (see Stated relations below).

**Predictor near-optimality (§5 "Importance of a near-optimal predictor", Appendix I):** removing the target network but using an optimal linear predictor (per-batch linear regression before back-propagation) avoids collapse, reaching 52.5% top-1; removing the target network but increasing only the predictor's learning rate reaches 66.5% top-1; increasing both projector and predictor learning rates without a target network gives ≈25% top-1 (poor).

# Applicability

- Use when: labels are scarce or absent, a ResNet-family backbone is being pretrained, and either batch size or the augmentation policy cannot be as aggressively controlled/scaled as a large-batch contrastive method would require — this is the paper's own headline differentiator (C3).
- Use when: downstream evaluation includes transfer to dense-prediction tasks (segmentation, detection, depth), not just linear-probe classification — the paper reports consistent gains there over both supervised-ImageNet and SimCLR baselines (Table 4).
- Don't use when: a validated queue-/memory-bank-based negative-pair infrastructure (e.g. MoCo) is already in place and batch-size/augmentation robustness is not the binding constraint.
- Compared against (within paper): SimCLR, MoCo / MoCo v2, CPC v2, CMC, PIRL, Local Aggregation, InfoMin Aug., DeepCluster (methods table, §4 Table 1/2/3; ablation comparisons in §5).

# Connections

- **Builds on:** the cross-view prediction framework (Doersch & Zisserman-style, cited as [63] in §3 opening) as the general lineage BYOL's prediction-in-representation-space setup descends from; SimCLR's augmentation pipeline and projection-head design, explicitly reused ("BYOL uses the same set of image augmentations as in SimCLR," "As in SimCLR, the representation $y$ is projected to a smaller space by a[n] MLP," §3.3); the moving-average target-network idea from deep RL (§2, citing Bellman-equation target-network stabilization work), explicitly credited as the inspiration for BYOL's EMA target; LARS optimizer (You et al., cited via [70]) and cosine LR decay (cited via [71]), reused unmodified from the large-batch-training toolkit SimCLR also used.
- **Contrastive baselines this paper positions against directly:** SimCLR (in-batch negatives, no target network, no predictor) and MoCo/MoCo v2 (queue + momentum-updated key encoder) — see Stated relations above for the paper's own framing of each.
- **Forward pointer — DINO (Caron et al. 2021, self-distillation lineage).** Not read for this note (no `caron2021-dino` research note exists yet in this repo). BYOL's online/target-with-EMA-and-stop-gradient design is widely understood in the broader SSL literature as a direct conceptual precursor to DINO's teacher/student self-distillation framework, which drops BYOL's explicit predictor MLP in favor of a sharpening/centering mechanism on the teacher's output distribution. No claims about DINO's own numbers or mechanism are made here — flagged as a forward pointer for the `self-supervised-learning` survey concept page to develop once a `caron2021-dino` note exists.

- **Consistency mechanism:** target network is an EMA of the online network, $\xi \leftarrow \tau\xi + (1-\tau)\theta$, $\tau_{\text{base}}=0.996$ cosine-annealed to 1 (main setup) — structurally similar to MoCo's momentum encoder but repurposed to stabilize a regression target rather than to keep a negative-key dictionary consistent.

# Provenance

- Abstract / §1 Introduction: "BYOL achieves higher performance than state-of-the-art contrastive methods without using negative pairs"; three enumerated contributions (i)/(ii)/(iii); "BYOL reaches 74.3% top-1 accuracy with a standard ResNet-50 and 79.6% top-1 accuracy with a larger ResNet"; ablation procedure "predicting a fixed randomly initialized network... achieve[s] 18.8% top-1 accuracy... whereas the randomly initialized network only achieves 1.4% by itself."
- §2 Related work: DeepCluster description ("uses bootstrapping on previous versions of its representation..."); PBL comparison ("BYOL uses a slow-moving average of its representation to provide its targets, and does not require a second network"); Mean Teacher description (softmax consistency loss, teacher/student terminology, "a similar approach collapses when removing the classification loss"); MoCo description ("uses a slow-moving average network (momentum encoder) to maintain consistent representations of negative pairs drawn from a memory bank. Instead, BYOL uses a moving average network to produce prediction targets... We show in Section 5 that this mere stabilizing effect can also improve existing contrastive methods").
- §3 (opening): motivation for bootstrapping; fixed-random-network baseline (18.8% vs. 1.4%).
- §3.1 "Description of BYOL": online/target network definitions, three-stage architecture (encoder/projector/predictor), Eq. 1 ($\xi \leftarrow \tau\xi + (1-\tau)\theta$), Eq. 2 (normalized MSE loss), symmetrization, Eq. 3 (optimizer update), stop-gradient statement ("with respect to $\theta$ only, but not $\xi$"), "At the end of training, we only keep the encoder $f_\theta$."
- §3.2 "Intuitions on BYOL's behavior": GAN analogy for the no-joint-loss hypothesis; "we did not observe convergence to such equilibria in our experiments"; Eq. 4 (optimal-predictor definition); Eq. 5 (conditional-variance gradient identity) with derivation pointer to Appendix H; collapsed-constant-instability argument.
- §3.3 "Implementation details": augmentation set description (crop, flip, color distortion, blur, solarization); ResNet-50 v1 base encoder, deeper/wider variants; projector architecture (linear-4096-BN-ReLU-linear-256, output not batch-normalized unlike SimCLR); predictor shares $g_\theta$'s architecture; LARS + cosine decay, 1000 epochs, 10-epoch warmup, base LR 0.2 scaled as $0.2\times\text{BatchSize}/256$, weight decay $1.5\times10^{-6}$ excluding biases/BN; $\tau_{\text{base}}=0.996$ cosine-annealed to 1, formula $\tau \triangleq 1-(1-\tau_{\text{base}})(\cos(\pi k/K)+1)/2$; batch 4096 on 512 TPU v3 cores, ~8h training.
- §4 "Experimental evaluation" / Table 1: linear-eval ImageNet numbers (BYOL 74.3/91.6 ResNet-50; 79.6/94.8 ResNet-200 2×; comparison rows SimCLR 69.3/89.0, MoCo v2 71.1, InfoMin Aug. 73.0/91.1, CPC v2 63.8/85.3, CMC 66.2/87.0, PIRL 63.6, Local Agg. 60.2); "a 1.3% (resp. 0.5%) improvement over the previous self-supervised state of the art"; supervised baselines 76.5% and 78.9% cited for context.
- §4 "Semi-supervised training on ImageNet" / Table 2: 1%/10%-label top-1/top-5 numbers for supervised, InstDisc, PIRL, SimCLR, BYOL (ResNet-50 and wider variants); "BYOL reaches 77.7% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-50 when fine-tuning over 100% of ImageNet labels."
- §4 "Transfer to other classification tasks" / Table 3: 12-dataset linear-eval and fine-tuned numbers; "BYOL outperforms SimCLR on all benchmarks and the Supervised-IN baseline on 7 of the 12 benchmarks."
- §4 "Transfer to other vision tasks" / Table 4: VOC2012 segmentation (+1.9 mIoU vs. supervised, +1.1 vs. SimCLR), VOC detection AP50 (+3.1 vs. supervised, +2.3 vs. SimCLR), NYU v2 depth (pct.<1.25: +3.5 vs. supervised, +1.3 vs. SimCLR).
- §5 "Building intuitions with ablations" (opening): 300-epoch/64-TPU-core ablation regime, initial LR 0.3, weight decay $10^{-6}$, $\tau_{\text{base}}=0.99$; rationale for using 300 epochs instead of 100.
- §5 "Batch size", Fig. 3a: "the performance of SimCLR rapidly deteriorates with batch size... In contrast, the performance of BYOL remains stable over a wide range of batch sizes from 256 to 4096, and only drops for smaller values due to batch normalization layers in the encoder"; footnote 7, "the only dependency on batch size in our training pipeline sits within the batch normalization layers."
- §5 "Image augmentations", Fig. 3b: color-distortion-removal ablation (−9.1 BYOL vs. −22.2 SimCLR); crops-only ablation (BYOL 72.5%→59.4%, SimCLR 67.9%→40.3%).
- §5 "Bootstrapping", Table 5a: target-decay-rate ablation table ($\tau_{\text{base}} \in \{1, 0.999, 0.99, 0.9\}$ and stop-gradient row) with top-1 numbers.
- §5 "Ablation to contrastive methods", Eq. 6 ($\text{InfoNCE}^{\alpha,\beta}_\theta$ generalized objective), Eq. 7 (normalized dot-product similarity $S_\theta$), Table 5b (predictor/target-network/$\beta$ ablation grid); "The only variant that performs well without negative examples (i.e., with $\beta=0$) is BYOL"; "Simply adding a target network to SimCLR already improves performance (+1.6 points). This sheds new light on the use of the target network in MoCo."
- §5 "Network hyperparameters": "removing the weight decay in either BYOL or SimCLR leads to network divergence"; init-scaling ablation (>72% top-1 regardless).
- §5 "Relationship with Mean Teacher": MT description, predictor-removal-collapses-to-MT-analogue argument, "Row 7 of Table 5," "the additional predictor is critical to prevent collapse."
- §5 "Importance of a near-optimal predictor": optimal-linear-predictor-without-target-network (52.5% top-1), increased-predictor-LR-without-target-network (66.5% top-1), increased-projector-and-predictor-LR-without-target-network (≈25% top-1); pointer to Appendix I.
- §6 Conclusion: "bridges most of the remaining gap between self-supervised methods and the supervised learning baseline"; "79.6%... improves over the previous state of the art (76.8%) while using 30% fewer parameters."
- Appendix B "Image augmentations", Table 6: full augmentation parameter table, including asymmetric Gaussian-blur probabilities (1.0 for $\mathcal{T}$, 0.1 for $\mathcal{T}'$) and asymmetric solarization probabilities (0.0 for $\mathcal{T}$, 0.2 for $\mathcal{T}'$); crop area $[8\%,100\%]$, aspect ratio $[3/4,4/3]$ log-uniform, bicubic resize to $224\times224$; color-jitter intensities (brightness/contrast 0.4, saturation 0.2, hue 0.1, $p=0.8$); color-dropping $p=0.2$, luma formula $0.2989r+0.5870g+0.1140b$; Gaussian-blur kernel $23\times23$, $\sigma\sim\text{Uniform}[0.1,2.0]$.
